# M6.4 — The library: every Commander-legal card, including the ones that are genuinely hard

**The goal, stated plainly.** The bot plays *the format*: all 31,692 distinct
Commander-legal cards, executed completely by the engine, with no card sitting in
a "the player applies this by hand" tier. Written 2026-07-31, after M6.3's
measurement (D127). **Nothing here is built.**

> **Read first:** `AGENTS.md` (the scope-tier table and the nine traps),
> `docs/DECISIONS.md` **D90** (never half-execute — the rule everything below
> obeys), **D121** (what the engine runs today, measured), **D122/D124** (a
> category left unenforced must be SAID), **D127** (what each missing primitive
> is worth), and `docs/M6-BOT-HANDOFF.md` §6 (which sketched this and
> under-counted it).

this milestone writes the scripts. `1,405` moves only here.

## 2. The rule that governs all of it

**Never half-execute (D90).** A script either runs every word of its card or it
is not registered. At one card this is a principle; at thirty thousand it is an
architecture, because the failure mode changes character:

- A **missing** card is honest and visible. `tier3.ts` says on the card what the
  app will not do, the bot refuses to draw it, and the player knows.
- A **wrong** card is silent. It corrupts a board state nobody can audit, and at
  this scale it will be found by a player mid-game rather than by a test.

Everything in §5 and §6 exists to keep the second number at zero, and every
mechanism below is allowed to cost coverage to do it.

⚠️ **Coverage may never be claimed by the thing that produces it.** The count of
executable cards comes from `engineComplete.ts` — the same predicate the bot's
deck pool and `tier3.ts`'s disclosure use — never from "how many scripts have we
landed". Those two numbers diverging is itself the alarm.

## 4. The hard classes, one at a time

This is the part the milestone lives or dies on. Each is a real engine project,
not a script.

### 4.1 Layers (CR 613) — the deepest

`derive.ts` runs layers 1, 7b, 7c and 7d. The full system is 1 (copy), 2
(control), 3 (text), 4 (type), 5 (colour), 6 (abilities), 7a (characteristic-
defining P/T), 7b (setting), 7c (modifying), 7d (counters), 7e (switching).

Two orderings, and the second is the hard one:

1. **Timestamp** — straightforward, and covers almost every real game.
2. **Dependency (CR 613.8)** — effect A depends on B when applying B changes what
   A applies to, or what A does. Then A waits for B regardless of timestamp.

⚠️ **Dependency is where correctness goes to die.** Humility + Opalescence is the
canonical case and has a published, counter-intuitive answer; Blood Moon +
Urborg, Conspiracy + Olivia, Sudden Spoiling + Overrun are all real. A naive
timestamp-only implementation is *right* for the overwhelming majority of boards
and *silently wrong* for these.

**Strategy.** Build timestamps first and ship them. Add dependency as a second
pass with a cycle guard (CR 613.8c: if effects depend on each other in a loop,
use timestamps). Keep a **conformance corpus** of the known-hard interactions
with their published answers, and treat a corpus failure as a release blocker.
Until dependency lands, a card whose script participates in a dependency-capable
effect stays unregistered — the D90 rule applied to a rules subsystem.

### 4.2 Cost modification (CR 601.2f) — the largest class, 1,496

Order is fixed and matters: apply cost *increases* first, then *reductions*, then
anything that sets the cost, then pay. Additional costs, alternative costs
("without paying its mana cost"), and the resource-substituting keywords
(Convoke, Delve, Improvise, Emerge) all land here.

⚠️ **This is the class that collides with existing work.** `payment.ts`'s
three-tier solver and its MCMF stage assume a *fixed* cost built by
`buildPaymentProblem`. Convoke changes what may pay; Delve changes the cost from
a zone. That is not a new effect kind — it is a change to the shape of the
payment problem, and it has to keep the property D53 records: a player must never
approve one payment and be charged another.

**Strategy.** Model cost modification as a pipeline of typed transformations over
the payment problem, computed in CR order, applied on the CLIENT for the preview
and re-derived on the HOST for the charge — the same "one solver, two callers"
discipline `previewCast` already has. Benchmark it: the 40-source board is at
0.100 ms today and that number is load-bearing.

### 4.3 Replacement and prevention (CR 614/615) — 1,154 + 491

`applyReplacements` exists with three built-ins (commander zone, entry counters,
transform counters). The general form needs:

- a registry of replacement effects keyed by event kind (the `ReplacementDef` API
  exists and ships empty),
- **CR 616's choice**: when several apply to one event, the affected player — or
  the affected object's controller — chooses the order. **That is a prompt**, and
  it is a real decision that changes outcomes.
- self-replacement applied before others,
- prevention as its own class, because a prevention shield is consumed.

⚠️ **A replacement must not re-trigger itself.** The API comment already says so;
at scale it needs a guard rather than a convention.

### 4.4 Copy effects (CR 707) — 980

"Copiable values" are defined recursively and deliberately exclude counters,
damage, and most continuous effects — but *include* copy effects already applied,
and other-face choices. Clones, token copies, and copies of spells on the stack
are three different mechanisms wearing one word.

**Strategy.** One `copiableValues(state, id)` primitive with the CR 707.2 list
encoded explicitly, and every clone script expressed in terms of it. No script may
compute copiable values itself.

### 4.5 Linked abilities (CR 607) — 621

"Exile it. You may play that card." The second ability must know exactly what the
first one exiled, across a zone change that CR 400.7 says makes a new object.

**Strategy.** A per-object `linkedMemory` on `GameState`, written by the first
ability's event and read by the second, keyed by the ability's own id. It must be
part of the state hash, or replay diverges.

### 4.6 Combat restrictions and requirements (CR 508.1) — 645

"Must attack each combat if able", "can't block creatures with power 3 or
greater", "must be blocked if able". CR 508.1d requires the declaration to
**maximise the number of requirements met without violating any restriction** —
a combinatorial optimisation over the attack/block assignment, not a filter.

⚠️ `canAttack`, `canBlock` and `legalDefenders` are per-creature predicates today.
The maximisation rule is a property of the whole declaration, exactly as menace
already is (D121 records the bot's blind spot there). This is the one class where
the *prompt payload* has to change: the host would have to ship legal
declarations rather than legal pairs, or validate a proposed one and explain the
refusal.

### 4.7 Hidden information and randomness — 688

The engine's seeded PRNG is threaded through the log already, so randomness is
solved. The hard half is **hidden information crossing `project()`**: a face-down
creature is a 2/2 with no name to opponents, its real identity to its controller,
and its morph cost is a secret until it is turned up. Getting that wrong leaks the
one thing `project()` exists to protect.

**Strategy.** Extend the existing `revealedTo` mechanism rather than adding a
second one, and add a leak assertion per new hidden class to the fuzz gate's
existing projection test.

### 4.8 Variable duration — 507

"For as long as", "until you lose control of", "as long as you control". A
continuous effect whose end is a *condition*, not a timestamp — it must be
re-evaluated whenever the condition's inputs change, and it can end mid-resolution.

### 4.9 Rule-changing cards — 166

Platinum Angel ("you can't lose the game"), "play with the top card of your
library revealed", "skip your draw step", Panglacial Wurm (cast a spell *while
searching*). These do not add an effect — they change the loop.

**Strategy.** A small, enumerated set of **rules hooks** with explicit names
(`canLose`, `skipStep`, `playFromZone`, `castDuringSearch`), each read at exactly
one place in `loop.ts`/`sba.ts`. No card may add a hook; a card may only set one.
An unbounded hook mechanism is how a rules engine becomes unauditable.

### 4.10 The ones that will never be automated

Named here so "all cards" has an honest footnote:

- **Subgames** (Shahrazad) — banned in Commander; out of scope by rule.
- **Cards requiring physical or out-of-game action** (dexterity, table talk,
  "shuffle a card in from outside the game" beyond a defined sideboard).
- **Cards whose text is genuinely ambiguous without a judge**, which exist and
  are documented per-card.

These stay Tier 3, keep their `tier3.ts` note, and are **listed by name in the
repo** rather than left as a rounding error.

## 5. The pipeline

```
scripts/cardgen/
  select.cjs   pick the next batch          (ordering: §7)
  draft.cjs    an LLM drafts a CardScript   DEV ONLY — never shipped, never at runtime
  verify.cjs   compile · lint · generated tests · conformance corpus · fuzz canary
  land.cjs     write accepted scripts into src/engine/scripts/cards/
```

Non-negotiable properties:

- **The output is ordinary reviewed TypeScript** that ships in the bundle. The
  model is a code generator used at build time. **The running game never touches
  a network and never calls a model** — the offline-first policy is absolute, and
  determinism, replay, rewind and the fuzz gate all depend on it.
- **Every generated script arrives with tests written against the REAL Scryfall
  oracle text** from the local database, never a paraphrase. D15b's rule.
- **A script that cannot be verified is not landed.** No exceptions, no "mostly
  works" tier.
- **Scripts return events.** They are never handed a mutation API. `ctx.random`
  is a seeded scratch RNG, so a coin-flip card replays bit-exactly.
- ⚠️ **The generator may never touch `src/engine/`.** It writes card scripts and
  their tests. Primitives are hand-written, reviewed work — that is where
  correctness for *every* card is decided.

## 6. Verification — the part that actually matters

Five gates, each catching something the others cannot.

1. **Per-card tests from real oracle text.** Generated with the script, reviewed
   with it, and asserting on EVENTS rather than on final state where possible.
2. **The conformance corpus.** A growing set of known-hard interactions with
   published answers — layer dependency, replacement ordering, copy values,
   combat maximisation. A corpus failure blocks a release. This is the only thing
   that catches a script that is individually right and wrong in combination.
3. **The 500-seed replay fuzz gate**, with **every landed card seeded into the
   pool**. Its own comment records the rule and this repo has broken it four
   times (D102, D107, D108, D121): a card missing from the pool is a code path
   the gate cannot reach, and the gate stays green the whole time it rots.
4. **`checkInvariants` after every intent**, which already runs in the fuzzer.
5. **Coverage accounting that cannot lie.** When a script lands, that card's
   `tier3.ts` note must go silent *and* `engineComplete` must accept it, in the
   same commit. A test asserts the two agree over the whole database — the
   relationship D122 established, scaled.

⚠️ **Report coverage as a measured number every batch**, in this repo's style:
*"X of 31,692 Commander-legal cards now execute completely, up from Y."* Never as
a count of scripts written.

## 7. Ordering

Cover what gets played first. Popularity data is an internet dependency and the
offline policy applies: **stop and ask the user before fetching any.**

Until then, the defensible offline proxy, in order:
1. Every card in the user's own saved decks (`~/.commanders-roundtable/decks/`).
2. Every card in the fuzz `DECK` and `ENGINE_CARDS` fixtures.
3. Cards reachable from the format's staples by colour identity and mana value —
   derivable from what is already on disk.
4. Then by difficulty tier, cheapest first, so each batch's verification cost is
   predictable.

## 9. What "done" means

**Done is not 31,692.** Done is:

- every card either **executes completely** or is on the **named** exclusion list
  of §4.10,
- the conformance corpus passes,
- the fuzz gate is green with every landed card in the pool,
- `engineComplete`, `tier3.ts` and the bot's deck pool all agree, over the whole
  database, in one test,
- and the bot's deck can be built from the format rather than from a curated
  subset — which is the point of all of it.

⚠️ Coverage is asymptotic and the last few hundred cards will cost more than the
first ten thousand. Say the number honestly at every batch, including when it
moves less than hoped.
